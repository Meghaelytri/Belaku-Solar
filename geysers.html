Instant or storage? Pick by where it goes

Instant heats as water flows — fast, never runs out, but a thin stream. Right for a kitchen tap, wrong for a bath.

Storage heats a tankful and holds it. Takes a few minutes, then gives a proper flow. Right for a bathroom.

Questions about geysers

Straight answers.

Still have questions?
What size geyser do I need for a bathroom?

For one or two people a 10 to 15 litre storage geyser is usually enough for a bucket bath or a short shower. Where several people bathe one after another, 25 litres avoids the wait for a reheat. Instant geysers of 3 to 5 litres suit kitchen taps rather than bathrooms.

Instant or storage geyser — which is better?

Neither is better in general; they suit different taps. Instant heats water as it flows and never runs out but gives a thin stream, which suits a kitchen sink. Storage heats a tankful and gives a proper flow, which suits a bathroom.

Does a geyser work with an overhead tank?

Most do, but very low overhead tanks produce weak pressure, and some geysers need a minimum head to operate properly. Tell us your tank height and we will point you to a pressure-rated model if you need one.

Should I buy a geyser if I already have a solar water heater?

Often yes, as a monsoon backup. A small geyser costs far less than upsizing a solar system, and it covers the handful of cloudy mornings a year when solar alone falls short. A hybrid solar system with a digital controller does the same job automatically if you would rather not think about it.
