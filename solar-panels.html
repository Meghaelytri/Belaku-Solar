On-grid or off-grid?

On-grid has no batteries. It cuts your monthly bill, costs less and needs almost no maintenance — but it shuts down during a power cut.

Off-grid stores power in batteries so the house keeps running. It costs considerably more, and the batteries are a consumable.

For most homes here the better answer is on-grid plus a separate UPS. It usually costs less than full off-grid, and each part does the job it's good at.

BillOn-gridNo batteries. Lowest cost per unit saved.
Power cutsOff-grid or UPSBatteries keep the house running.
Roof~100 sq ft per kWUnshaded. We survey free before quoting.

There's a third option: hybrid

  • Grid-connected, but with a battery too
  • Costs more than on-grid alone, less than full off-grid
  • Keeps essentials running during a cut, unlike plain on-grid

The central government's rooftop solar subsidy scheme for homes. It covers a share of the installation cost on a grid-connected system, with the exact amount set by the slabs in effect when you apply.

Questions about rooftop solar panels

Straight answers.

Still have questions?
Should I choose an on-grid or off-grid rooftop solar system?

Choose on-grid if the goal is a lower bill and your supply is reasonably stable, since there are no batteries and maintenance is lower. Choose off-grid if cuts are frequent and the house must keep running through them. Many homes are better served by on-grid plus a separate UPS.

How much roof space does a home solar system need?

Roughly 100 square feet of unshaded roof per kilowatt, so a typical 3 kW home system needs around 300 square feet. The roof also has to be free of shade for most of the day, which is what the free survey checks.

Will rooftop solar run my house during a power cut?

An on-grid system will not, because it shuts down automatically during a cut for the safety of line workers. Only off-grid or hybrid systems with batteries keep running, which is why many homes pair on-grid solar with a separate UPS.

How long does a rooftop solar system last?

Panels typically carry performance warranties measured in decades and the inverter is usually the first component to need replacing. Batteries, where fitted, are the shortest-lived part, which is one more reason on-grid systems need less attention over time.
